Questions from independent consultants

A few things people usually want to know before applying.

Do I need an LLC to apply, or can I work as a sole proprietor?+

You can apply as a sole proprietor — no formal entity required to submit an application. Many independents do form an LLC or S-Corp once work becomes steady, mainly for liability protection, but that's a decision worth making with an accountant on your own timeline, not a prerequisite here.

What's SAM.gov registration, and do I need it before I apply?+

SAM.gov is the federal government's registry of entities eligible for contract awards — free, but it can take a couple of weeks to clear. You don't need it to apply; we only ask for your SAM.gov Unique Entity ID (UEI) during vetting, after you're approved.

If you don't have one yet, that's fine — just know you won't be matched to opportunities until it's complete, so it's worth starting the process as soon as you're invited to be vetted.

What's the difference between FFP, FFP-LOE, and T&M contracts — and why does it matter to me?+

FFP (Firm-Fixed-Price) pays a set price for a defined deliverable, regardless of hours.

FFP-LOE (Firm-Fixed-Price, Level-of-Effort) pays a fixed price for a specified level of effort over a set period, rather than a deliverable.

T&M (Time-and-Materials) pays for actual hours at an agreed rate.

It matters because it changes how you scope and price your time — we work to convert T&M-style requirements into FFP or FFP-LOE wherever possible, since those tend to be simpler for independents to manage.

How does Responsa's vetting process work?+

After you apply, we review your initial submission; approved applicants get a short follow-up form for more detail (resume link, SAM.gov UEI, clearance status, relevant contract-type experience). Once that's in, you get a real account and show up as available in our network. Nothing about you is shared with agencies before that point.

What about health insurance and benefits as an independent?+

You're generally responsible for your own coverage — the government doesn't provide benefits to contractors directly. Common paths are ACA marketplace plans, a spouse's employer plan, or a professional-association group plan. For your specific situation, a licensed health insurance broker is the most reliable source.
