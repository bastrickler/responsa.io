Responsa T&M → FPLOE/FFP conversion — internal tool

Paste a task order that's currently priced as Time-and-Materials (T&M) labor-hour. The pipeline breaks it into work elements and, for each one, recommends and drafts either a Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) deliverable, a Firm-Fixed-Price Level-of-Effort (FPLOE) structure, or — only when genuinely warranted — the justification required to keep it as T&M.

Quick reference, before you start

T&M
Time-and-Materials / labor-hour. You pay for hours worked, regardless of what gets produced. FAR 16.601 says this should only be used when it truly isn't possible to estimate the extent or duration of the work in advance — it requires a signed determination, not just habit.
FFP
Firm-Fixed-Price. You pay a set price for a defined deliverable, checked against a stated acceptance criterion. Best fit when the output can be described well enough that someone could say "done" or "not done."
FPLOE
Firm-Fixed-Price, Level-of-Effort (FAR 16.207). You pay a fixed price for a known, capped amount of effort over a period — a middle ground for real advisory or support work where the output can't be fully pre-defined, but the amount of effort can be. FAR is explicit that FPLOE still pays for effort expended, not results achieved.
CLIN / SLIN
A CLIN (contract line item) is where the T&M-vs-FFP switch actually lives. A SLIN (subline item) lets you split one CLIN's work into separately priced, separately delivered pieces without standing up a whole new CLIN structure.

None of this is legal advice — your contracting officer makes the final contract-type determination. This tool is meant to give you a defensible first draft to bring to that conversation, not to replace it.

Stage 1 — Segment

Splits a T&M task order into discrete work elements, without yet deciding contract type.

Stage 2 — Classify by contract type

Applies the FAR 16.601 test to recommend FFP, FPLOE, or (rarely) that T&M is genuinely required.

Stage 3 — Draft structure

Drafts the deliverables/milestones (FFP), level-of-effort structure (FPLOE), or FAR 16.601 justification (T&M) for one element.

Stage 4 — Lint

Flags boilerplate T&M justifications, vague FFP criteria, or FPLOE structures that quietly grade outputs instead of effort.

Stage 5 — Readiness check

Checks the task order for the six fields that gate a consultant match (clearance, citizenship, location/on-site vs. remote, price/rate ceiling, availability window, degree requirement) and drafts a plain-language reminder for anything missing.

Internal tool. AI drafts every contract-type recommendation; a human reviews and edits before anything goes to a contracting officer. Contract-type recommendations (FFP/FPLOE/T&M) are a starting draft for your contracting officer's determination, not a substitute for it.
