Questions from independent consultants

A few things people usually want to know before applying.

Do I need an LLC to apply, or can I work as a sole proprietor?+

You can apply as a sole proprietor — no formal entity required to submit an application. Many independents do form an LLC or S-Corp once work becomes steady, mainly for liability protection.

What's SAM.gov registration, and do I need it before I apply?+

SAM.gov is the federal government's registry of entities eligible for contract awards — free, but it can take a couple of weeks to clear. You don't need it to apply; we only ask for your SAM.gov Unique Entity ID (UEI) during vetting, after you're approved.

What's the difference between FFP, FFP-LOE, and T&M contracts — and why does it matter to me?+

FFP (Firm-Fixed-Price) pays a set price for a defined deliverable, regardless of hours.

FFP-LOE (Firm-Fixed-Price, Level-of-Effort) pays a fixed price for a specified level of effort over a set period, rather than a deliverable.

T&M (Time-and-Materials) pays for actual hours at an agreed rate.

It matters because it changes how you scope and price your time — we work to convert T&M-style requirements into FFP or FFP-LOE wherever possible.

How does Responsa's vetting process work?+

After you apply, we review your initial submission; approved applicants get a short follow-up form for more details. Once that's in, you get a real account and show up as available in our network. Nothing about you is shared with agencies before that point.

What about health insurance and benefits as an independent?+

You're generally responsible for your own coverage as an independent consultant. Common paths are ACA marketplace plans, a spouse's employer plan, or a professional-association group plan. A licensed health insurance broker is often the most reliable source.

How much should I charge?+

A common starting point: double your expected salary and divide by 250 (roughly a year's working days) for a very rough daily rate. The doubling covers costs a salaried employee doesn't see — benefits, overhead, non-billable time.

More detailed guides on all of this are available to members.
